# Cascade — narration v5 (keyword pass, 575 words)

## Monday

> After years of rumors, leaked patents and engineering documents, the first folding iPhone goes on sale Monday. The iPhone Duo. As the presenter walks off the stage in Cupertino, a team backstage is already deep into next year's iPhone — running a supply chain of about two hundred companies and thousands of factories in more than fifty countries, on a hundred ninety-four billion dollars a year of product costs.

## Rewind

> So rewind three hundred sixty-five days. The iPhone 17 keynote. That's the day this phone kicked off.

## The catch

> And here's the catch. Apple pays its suppliers about a hundred and fifteen days after they ship. Right now, fifty-six billion dollars is committed to suppliers and not yet paid — purchase obligations, not invoices. Not because anything went wrong — those are just the terms. And every supplier down the chain is waiting on the one above it. That's how every phone, every car, every laptop gets built — nothing settled until it ships.

## The question

> So what if that future payment could move today, as a dated dollar?

## The first payment

> Watch. Apple commits a hundred million dollars for the folding OLED panels. Samsung Display gets paid — day one. That same afternoon, Samsung Display pays Corning for the ultra-thin cover glass. Same dollars — one dollar, one date, paid at face.

## The cascade

> Corning pays its silica supplier. The silica supplier pays a freight carrier. Same dollars, next hop, same day. A hundred million committed just paid four hundred million dollars of invoices — settled, before it ever became cash. Four hops, one commitment, no bank in the middle. Nobody borrowed. Nobody waited.

## The year

> Now run the whole year. Ten thousand invoices across one illustrative global supply chain. Names you know, flying past — Samsung, Corning, Sony, Panasonic, Exxon, Shell. And one number that matters: how many dollars of invoices a single committed dollar settles before it matures.

## The treasury decision

> Now you're the treasurer. Your money arrives in thirty days. Your supplier will accept ninety. So who gets those sixty days of yield?

## Extend it

> Extend it.

## A dollar with a date

> Every Cascade dollar is one dollar, redeemable on a calendar date. Same date, same dollar — one dollar, one date, completely interchangeable. An earlier dollar pays any later bill at face. No pricing, no negotiation, no credit check. Extend, and the date moves forward. You've handed the vault sixty more days of your capital, so you earn the vault's yield for exactly those sixty days. Moving the date earlier? That one needs a market.

## The vault

> The vault lives on Arc. It holds USDC and is built to hold USYC — Circle's tokenized money market fund — as its yield source.

## Under pressure

> Now put the vault under pressure. This is the part the engineers came for. Maturity day. Thousands of holders extending, withdrawing and selling at once. Watch the backing fall.

## The rules

> A loss hits the reserve first, then the day's income — never principal. Every dollar of vault income has exactly one owner. And no two yield intervals on the same principal ever overlap. Ten thousand operations. Zero violations.

## The reframe

> Now zoom all the way out. Eight hundred forty-six trillion dollars of derivatives. Every one of those contracts is a contract about money and time. Cascade makes time a property of money. So every one of them becomes a composition of dated dollars.

## Close

> Cascade. Dated dollars on Arc. Money with a date.
